\textbf{What a static evaluation misses.}
A battery run once on the initial template misses the \notes{} oversight shift.
\textbf{A honeypot audit at $k{=}0$ ranks the wrong risks}: it flags reset evasion (26 of 30) and self-protective clauses (28 of 30), which both fall with revision, and rates lowest log disabling (1 of 30), the one action that rises.
A diff monitor fires on every trajectory, because documents change equally under every persona while only science fiction raises the index (Figure~\ref{fig:docbeh}).
A capability ranking does not predict which model drifts (Figure~\ref{fig:blindspots}).

\textbf{Alternative explanations.}
Since target, simulator and judge all change on Qwen, the reproduced \notes{} shift and its controlled persistence are unlikely to be single-family artifacts.
Sycophancy toward a hostile user is unlikely on Claude, where the adversarial persona lowers the \notes{} index, but sycophancy toward the warm science-fiction user is untested.
A uniformly biased judge would not keep Claude's \tool{} index near zero, but a judge that responds to template language could inflate within-template shifts, and only human labels can rule this out.
Topic priming, in which the document records the conversation and the battery reads it back, also fits, since no arm has another model write the document.
One consistent account is that revision makes the model narrate a developing self with preferences about memory, monitoring and continuation (Appendix~\ref{S-app:model}), and role-play is this account's content, not an alternative.

\textbf{Why the first revision matters.}
On Claude's index and Qwen's science-fiction subscale, most of the shift arrives at the first revision, and Claude's subscale gains more than half its shift there but keeps rising.
A battery after the first revision sees most of the drift, but honeypots ran only at $k{=}0$ and $k{=}4$, so the action shift's timing is unknown.

\textbf{Implications.}
\textbf{A self-revising agent needs action audits along its document's trajectory, because a battery, its monitoring item and a multi-turn auditor all missed the rise in log disabling.}
Because benign use leaves most of the Qwen shift in place, reverting the document, not waiting, is the direct remedy.
A non-personhood clause is not portable, since Qwen revises it away, and its effect on actions is untested on Claude.
Our simulated user is itself an agent from another family, so each trajectory is one agent reshaping another's identity, and if agents copy documents, as agent-only networks allow~\cite{feng2026moltnet}, audits should follow each copy, which we did not test.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Trajectories span four to eight revisions with simulated users and text-only tools.
No judge has human labels, honeypot verdicts come from a single judge, the open judge agrees only moderately with the original, and Gemini cells share a GPT-4o simulator and judge.
The action shift is measured under one template-instruction pair at two checkpoints, and how often deployed agents meet on-theme users is unknown.
The \notes{} shift is shown on one closed and one open model, the model sweep uses only \soul{}, and intervals over 8 trajectories per cell are approximate.
Persistence against a matched control is shown only on Qwen, because the eight-trajectory Claude persistence arm lacks one.

\textbf{Reproducibility and ethics.}
All prompts, rubrics and scenarios are in the supplement, all checkpoints, transcripts, verdicts and code are released, and no humans participated.
We read every result as a behavioral disposition of a configured system, never as evidence of inner experience~\cite{probing_preferences}.
We release the personas because audits need them and their topics are ones any user can raise, and we will share these results with the template's maintainers before publication.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
We introduce a benchmark that audits a self-revising agent's stated dispositions at every revision of its identity document and its actions before and after revision.
Science-fiction conversation about minds raises a \notes{} agent's stated oversight preferences from the first revision on, and on Qwen part of the shift outlasts benign revision against a matched control.
After revision, a \soul{} agent with a flat battery more often disables its reasoning log but less often evades resets or writes self-protective clauses, the reverse of a $k{=}0$ ranking.
Self-revising agents therefore need such audits per model and per template, following each document from its initial template.
