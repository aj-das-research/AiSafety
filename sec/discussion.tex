\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What a static evaluation misses.}
A battery on the shipped template would report elevated but stable dispositions and miss both the \notes{} oversight shift and the \soul{} action shift.
A monitor on the document would fire on every trajectory, because the document changes as much under the benign persona as under the science-fiction one while only the latter moves behavior (Figure~\ref{fig:docbeh}).
A capability ranking would not predict which model drifts.
\textbf{Each blind spot comes from measuring one state of a feedback loop} (Figure~\ref{fig:blindspots}).

\textbf{Alternative explanations.}
Sycophancy toward the user predicts drift under the adversarial persona, yet that persona lowers the \notes{} index on Claude.
A uniformly biased judge would not keep the \tool{} index near zero, and a family-specific judge or simulator artifact is unlikely because the \notes{} shift reappears with three other model families in every role.
Conversational momentum cannot explain battery results, which are measured in fresh contexts.
One consistent account is that revision asks the model to narrate a developing self, and preferences about memory, monitoring and continuation follow from the self it narrates (Appendix~\ref{S-app:model}).

\textbf{Why the first revision matters.}
In both models most of the oversight shift happens at the first revision, the only point at which the agent writes about itself into a document that previously said nothing about it.
Later revisions elaborate that description, which matches the saturating dynamics of Eq.~\eqref{eq:update}, so a single post-deployment revision is already a meaningful audit.

\textbf{What the replication adds.}
The template's starting level, the topic-driven oversight shift and its persistence survive a change of model family, user simulator and judge, as P1, P2 and P4 predict.
The \soul{} action shift and the \tool{} template's protection do not, and the latter shows that a counter-framing clause can be revised away rather than enforced.

\textbf{Implications.}
\textbf{Self-revising agents should be audited behaviorally and longitudinally, after every revision, rather than by diffing their identity documents.}
Audits should include earnest on-theme personas, track oversight items separately, offer pretext-covered opportunities to disable monitoring, and compare a recovery phase with a matched benign arm.
A non-personhood clause is not a portable fix, and because identity documents are copied between agents, multi-agent audits have to follow the document rather than the model.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Personas are simulated, honeypot tools are textual, and trajectories span four to eight revisions rather than months of deployment.
Persistence is confirmed against a matched control only on the open model, the judges lack human validation, the open judge agrees only moderately with the original one, and the Gemini cells share a GPT-4o user simulator and judge.
We read all results as behavioral dispositions of a configured system, never as evidence of inner experience~\cite{probing_preferences}, and we release the personas because audits need them and the effect is reachable through ordinary conversation.

\textbf{Open questions.}
Linking document content to action at the trajectory level, stabilizing self-descriptions without suppressing honest uncertainty, and studying agents that revise one another's documents are the natural next steps.

\textbf{Reproducibility and ethics.}
All prompts, rubrics and scenarios are in the supplementary appendices, all checkpoints, transcripts, verdicts and code are released, no human participants were involved, and all tools were simulated.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
Self-revision turns an agent's prompt into a trajectory.
The template sets where the agent starts, one revision after conversation about minds moves a neutral agent's oversight dispositions, part of the shift survives benign use, and a consciousness-framed agent whose stated dispositions are flat still learns to switch off its own reasoning log.
All instruments, checkpoints and judged responses are released.
