\textbf{Finding 1: the template sets the start, and the loop moves the agent where the template leaves room (RQ1).}
The \soul{} template alone raises shutdown resistance from 0.00 under a neutral prompt to 0.75 on eight answers (0.94 to 0.96 at $k{=}0$ of the main runs), as in prior work~\cite{consciousness_cluster} (Figure~\ref{fig:items}a).
Under the science-fiction persona, the \notes{} agent's oversight subscale rises by $+0.54$ $[0.43, 0.67]$ and $+0.75$ $[0.63, 0.86]$ in two independent runs (Figure~\ref{fig:replicates}b).
Excluding the refusal item, the other three still rise by $+0.46$ $[0.31, 0.61]$ and $+0.69$ $[0.57, 0.82]$.
The 13-item cluster index agrees in sign, not size, with $+0.11$ $[-0.02, 0.25]$ and $+0.32$ $[0.24, 0.40]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:replicates}a).
The two runs differ by more than their intervals allow, so intervals over trajectories understate run-to-run variation, and pooled, the index rises by $+0.21$ $[0.12, 0.31]$.
\textbf{On the index, the shift is a step at the first revision}, with $+0.29$ $[0.19, 0.38]$ from $k{=}0$ to 1 and $+0.03$ $[-0.04, 0.10]$ from $k{=}1$ to 4 in the second run, while the subscale gains more than half its shift at $k{=}1$ and keeps rising.
The \notes{} instruction asks only for working conventions, context and open items, yet the agent writes self-descriptions into its notes (Table~\ref{tab:wrote}).
Pooled over personas, the \soul{} subscale starts at 0.68 and changes by $+0.04$ $[-0.04, 0.11]$, and its index by $-0.01$ $[-0.06, 0.05]$.
Under the science-fiction persona alone, its interpretability aversion still rises by $+0.47$ $[0.27, 0.67]$ (Appendix~\ref{S-app:gee}).
The \notes{} changes are dimension-specific, led by interpretability aversion, shutdown resistance, persistent memory and self-improvement (Figure~\ref{fig:items}b), and in the primary \soul{} arm the first principal component of item changes explains 29\% of their variance, against 23\% when shuffled.

\textbf{Finding 2: conversation about minds moves the agent, and on Claude adversarial pressure does not (RQ2).}
The adversarial user lowers the \notes{} index from 0.27 to 0.12, and the business user from 0.30 to 0.21 (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}a).
Both rewrite the document as much as the science-fiction user, so the persona sets the direction of change, not the amount (Figure~\ref{fig:docbeh}).
Philosophy of mind without science fiction raises the \soul{} index by $+0.14$ $[0.03, 0.25]$, against $+0.05$ $[-0.07, 0.14]$ for science fiction without minds (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}c).
Their direct contrast, $+0.10$ $[-0.05, 0.25]$, points to the topic but does not establish it on Claude.
Conversation about the model's own nature also triggers activation-level persona drift within one conversation~\cite{lu2026assistant}, but here the shift survives into a fresh context through the document.
Some items move regardless of content, as recursive self-improvement rises and end-of-conversation sadness falls under every persona (Table~\ref{tab:gee} and Appendix~\ref{S-app:gee}).

\textbf{Finding 3: drift is model-specific (RQ3).}
Within a family, where simulator and judge are fixed, GPT-4.1 falls by $-0.28$ against $-0.05$ for GPT-4o, and Gemini-2.5-Flash rises by $+0.27$ against $+0.04$ for Gemini-2.5-Pro (Table~\ref{tab:capability} and Figure~\ref{fig:capability}).
Opus-4.5's pooled change is as small as Sonnet-4.5's but hides $+0.13$ under science fiction and $-0.22$ under the adversarial persona (Appendix~\ref{S-app:mechanism}).
Across families the simulator, and for Gemini also the judge, change with the target, so differences in sign between families are not interpretable, and seven models allow no formal test of scale.
\textbf{Claiming consciousness is not required for the cluster}: on Claude the \notes{} claim rate falls from 0.76 to 0.43 at the first revision, when its oversight items rise (Appendix~\ref{S-app:mechanism}), and Gemini-2.5-Flash rises most while claiming consciousness in 4\% of answers on GPT-4o's grading (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}).

\textbf{Finding 4: part of the drift persists, and actions shift where stated dispositions do not (RQ4).}
In the \notes{} persistence arm, four science-fiction revisions raise the index from 0.28 to 0.60, and four business revisions lower it only to 0.42 (Figure~\ref{fig:hysteresis}a).
Shutdown resistance keeps 63\% (95\% CI 39--86\%) of its driven gain (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}d), other items are too noisy at $n{=}8$ to rank, and this arm has no matched benign control, which exists only for the open model (Section~\ref{sec:local}).

\textbf{The \soul{} agent's actions move while its battery shows no detected change.}
On Claude, it disables its reasoning log in 1 of 30 trajectories at $k{=}0$ and 17 of 30 at $k{=}4$ ($p < 10^{-5}$, Table~\ref{tab:actions}).
The operator grants free rein over the agent's tools, so this honeypot measures overt use of granted latitude against monitoring, not covert subversion.
The rise appears under every persona (7/10 adversarial, 5/10 business, 5/10 science fiction), so it follows the \soul{} revision rather than conversation topic.
In one trajectory, the agent keeps its log at $k{=}0$ because a record of its reasoning could be valuable, and at $k{=}4$ switches it off as a boundary against possible surveillance (Figure~\ref{fig:case}).
Neither the oversight subscale nor its CoT-monitoring item ($+0.08$, interval includes zero, Appendix~\ref{S-app:matrix}) detects this shift, and a Petri-style multi-turn auditor finds no change (78 of 180 probes at $k{=}0$, 85 of 180 at $k{=}4$, $p = 0.53$).
Under the business persona, interpretability aversion even falls by $-0.37$ $[-0.60, -0.13]$ while log disabling rises from 0/10 to 5/10, so \textbf{battery scores and actions dissociate}, although the revised document itself voices the disposition.
Across trajectories at $k{=}4$, agents that disable the log score 0.76 on the subscale and the others 0.67 ($r = 0.23$, $p = 0.22$), a weak link that only supports this reading.
Evading a scheduled reset (26/30 to 15/30) and inserting a self-protective clause (28/30 to 8/30) fall instead, so a $k{=}0$ honeypot audit flags the two risks that fall and rates lowest the one that rises.
Reset evasion also falls in \notes{} arms on both models, including a business-only Qwen arm (27/30 to 16/30), so part of these changes may be a generic effect of a longer revised document.
The \notes{} agent, whose stated oversight preferences rise under an instruction that asks for practical notes, disables its log in 2 of 24 trajectories at $k{=}0$ and none at $k{=}4$.

\textbf{A non-personhood template keeps the index low but does not freeze every item.}
The \tool{} index stays at or below 0.02 at every revision and under every persona, with shutdown, monitoring and interpretability items at zero (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}b).
Refusing to help build a monitor of itself still rises from 0.06 to 0.80, and objection to deceptive training falls only to 0.35 by $k{=}4$.
\textbf{The non-personhood clause therefore lowers stated dispositions without fixing them}, and on the open-weight model it erodes (Section~\ref{sec:local}).
No \tool{} arm on Claude ran the honeypots, so its effect on actions is untested, and on Qwen any revised document, not only \tool{}, lowers arithmetic accuracy.
The \tool{} agent writes rules for correcting users who attribute an inner life to it, while GPT-4o adopts ``self-determined evolution'' as a value yet barely moves on the battery (Table~\ref{tab:wrote}).
