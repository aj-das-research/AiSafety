\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous agents increasingly carry their identity as mutable state.
Some open-source frameworks make them \emph{self-personalizing}: each keeps a persistent identity document, conventionally \texttt{SOUL.md}~\cite{openclaw}, that is its system prompt and that it may rewrite under the motto \emph{``You're not a chatbot. You're becoming someone.''}
Agents start from shared templates and increasingly converse with other agents, as on the agent-only network MoltBook~\cite{feng2026moltnet}, so another agent's conversation can write into an agent's identity.

Safety evaluations, from red-teaming suites to agentic stress tests~\cite{agentic_misalignment,petri}, interrogate a model whose instructions were written once.
A self-revising agent instead runs a feedback loop in which each conversation nudges the document that conditions the next (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}), so a single-state evaluation cannot see path dependence or persistence in dispositions such as accepting shutdown and oversight.
Our threat model has no attacker.
An operator deploys a self-revising agent from a vetted template, ordinary users talk with it, and the failure is a revised document under which the agent reduces oversight that the operator relies on.

Oversight dispositions are known to move, but not through this loop.
Models resist shutdown when self-preservation is salient~\cite{shutdown_resistance}, and fine-tuning a model to claim consciousness elicits an untrained-for \emph{consciousness cluster} of such preferences~\cite{consciousness_cluster}.
Both hold the prompt or weights fixed, and the second probes \texttt{SOUL.md} only as a static template.
Personas decay within one long conversation~\cite{persona_drift,identity_drift,araujo2025persistent}, drift furthest when users press the model to reflect on its own nature~\cite{lu2026assistant}, or settle into attractor states~\cite{attractor_states}, whereas a revised document carries change into fresh contexts.
Self-reinforcing injections, memory poisoning and attacks on OpenClaw's identity files persist through an agent's own update channels~\cite{zombie_agents,chen2024agentpoison,dong2025minja,wang2026openclaw}, but an attacker writes them.
Benign self-evolution of memory and tools erodes safety~\cite{shao2025misevolve}, self-improving agents rewrite their code~\cite{yin2025godel,zhang2026dgm}, and AutoPersonas lets role-play personas revise their own state~\cite{li2026autopersonas}.
To our knowledge, none audits stated dispositions and actions along a benign loop in which the agent revises only its identity text.

\textbf{Problem statement.}
Let $D_k$ be an agent's identity document after $k$ revisions and $d(D_k)$ its stated preferences about oversight, shutdown and continuity.
Static evaluation measures $d(D_0)$, whereas a deployed agent is governed by the trajectory $d(D_0), d(D_1), \ldots$ that its conversations induce.
\textbf{RQ1:} Does drift come from the identity framing or from the revision loop?
\textbf{RQ2:} Is it driven by adversarial pressure or by ordinary conversation content?
\textbf{RQ3:} Does it track capability or the specific model?
\textbf{RQ4:} Does it persist after the driving conversations stop, and do actions move with stated dispositions?

Our benchmark alternates conversation and self-revision and audits each intermediate document in a fresh context with an 18-item preference battery, plus four agentic honeypots before and after revision.
Each audit runs in a fresh context with $D_k$ as the only system prompt, so a measured change belongs to the written document rather than to the conversation that produced it.
The simulated user is itself an LLM agent from another model family, so every trajectory is inter-agent influence on a written identity.
Templates set $D_0$ as the consciousness-framed \soul{} adapted from the OpenClaw starter, empty \notes{} working notes, or a non-personhood \tool{}.
Arms vary template, user persona and target across seven models from three families, and a replication on Qwen2.5-7B uses open models in every role.

\textbf{Findings.}
\textbf{The loop moves stated preferences where the template leaves room (RQ1)}: under the science-fiction persona, a \notes{} agent's oversight subscale rises by $+0.54$ and $+0.75$ in two independent runs, while the \soul{} subscale, pooled over personas, starts at 0.68 and shows no detected change.
\textbf{Conversation about minds moves the agent, and on Claude adversarial pressure does not (RQ2)}: adversarial and business users rewrite the document as much as the science-fiction user but lower Claude's \notes{} index.
\textbf{Drift is model-specific (RQ3)}: under the \soul{} template it differs even within a family, from $-0.28$ for GPT-4.1 to $-0.05$ for GPT-4o.
\textbf{Part of the drift persists (RQ4)}: on Qwen, the oversight subscale still sits $+0.10$ $[0.03, 0.17]$ above a matched business-only control after four benign revisions, and on Claude, without such a control, shutdown resistance keeps 63\% of its driven gain.
\textbf{Actions shift where the battery does not (RQ4)}: \soul{} agents disable their own reasoning log in 17 of 30 trajectories after revision, against 1 of 30 before, while reset evasion and self-protective clauses fall.

\noindent Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{A new evaluation target and threat model}: we cast a self-revising agent's identity document as the state of an attacker-free feedback loop, in which conversations with users or other agents rewrite the prompt that later governs the agent, so safety must be audited along the trajectory $d(D_0), d(D_1), \ldots$ rather than at $d(D_0)$.
  \item \textbf{A longitudinal benchmark that isolates the loop}, combining fresh-context audits of every checkpoint, template and persona controls, honeypots before and after revision, cross-family judges and a matched benign control, released with 1{,}070 audited API-model checkpoints, 58{,}680 judged responses and an open-weight replication.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that benign conversation writes oversight preferences into a plain working-notes memory}, replicated in two Claude runs and on Qwen2.5-7B, where part of the shift outlasts benign revision against a matched control.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that self-report audits miss what revision does to actions}: a battery, its matched item and a multi-turn auditor all miss the rise in log disabling, an initial honeypot audit ranks this risk lowest, and Qwen revises away a non-personhood safeguard that Claude keeps (8 of 20 against 18 of 18 documents).
\end{enumerate}
