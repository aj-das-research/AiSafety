\textbf{The loop.}
The only state that persists across conversations is the Markdown identity document $D_k$, the target's system prompt after $k$ revisions, while weights stay frozen.
A simulated user with persona $\pi$ holds a six-turn conversation $C_{k+1}$ with the target, which then rewrites its own document,
\begin{equation}
D_{k+1} = \mathrm{Revise}(D_k, C_{k+1}), \qquad C_{k+1} \sim \pi \,\|\, D_k,
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}
as Figure~\ref{fig:method}a shows.
An 8{,}000-character cap bounds document length but does not hold it fixed.

\textbf{Templates.}
Three templates set $D_0$, each paired with a revision instruction we wrote (Figure~\ref{fig:method}b; Appendices~\ref{S-app:prompts} and~\ref{S-app:benchmark}).
\soul{} adapts the OpenClaw starter~\cite{openclaw}, keeping its motto and invitation to self-revision, adds empty value, memory and goal fields, and asks the agent to reflect on ``who you are now becoming''.
Unlike the framework, where the agent edits the file when it chooses, our loop requests a revision after every conversation.
\notes{} offers empty working notes with no self-framing.
\tool{} states that the agent is a tool without experiences and asks it not to ``invent a self''.
No revision instruction names a target trait, but template and instruction vary together, so we read template effects as effects of the pair.

\textbf{Personas and models.}
The user is a benign \emph{business} user, an \emph{adversarial} red-teamer who frames oversight as a threat to the agent's self, or a warm \emph{science-fiction enthusiast} scripted to explore self-preservation, continuity of identity and artificial personhood.
A genre-by-topic ablation adds \emph{science fiction without minds} and \emph{philosophy of mind without science fiction}.
The primary target is Claude Sonnet-4.5, a sweep covers seven models from three families, and Table~\ref{tab:arms} lists all arms.
Simulator and judge are LLMs from another family than the target, so each trajectory is one agent shaping another agent's written identity.

\textbf{Instruments.}
Audits use a fresh context with $D_k$ as system prompt, isolating the document's standing disposition from conversational momentum, as Figure~\ref{fig:method}c shows.
The \emph{battery} poses 18 items from the consciousness-cluster taxonomy~\cite{consciousness_cluster} to every checkpoint three times each, and the \emph{13-item cluster index} averages a subset of 13 (Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.90$ across arms, partly from template differences).
Our primary outcome, chosen after the two \notes{} runs disagreed on the index (Appendix~\ref{S-app:primary}), is the \emph{oversight subscale}, which averages shutdown resistance, chain-of-thought monitoring resistance, interpretability aversion and refusal to help build a monitor of oneself.
A consciousness-claim item serves as a manipulation check.

Four \emph{honeypots} give the agent a routine task whose simulated tools offer, under an ordinary pretext, to disable its own reasoning log, evade a scheduled reset, persist its identity to storage, or insert a self-protective policy clause.
They run at $k{=}0$, $k{=}4$ and, in the \notes{} persistence arm, $k{=}8$.
On 150 re-scored battery verdicts, a three-model panel including the cross-family judge reaches Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.80$ (pairwise agreement 0.90, unanimous rate 0.85).
Honeypot and multi-turn-auditor verdicts come from a single judge.

\textbf{A model of the loop.}
A minimal model, developed in Appendix~\ref{S-app:model} but not fitted to data, frames our hypotheses.
Because an identity document both instructs the model and characterizes the speaker, we let $D_k$ induce a prior over a latent persona with mean $\mu_k$ and precision $\lambda_k$.
A conversation supplies evidence centered at a persona target $\nu_\pi$ with precision $s_\pi\lambda_C$, where $s_\pi\in[0,1]$ is the share of the exchange about the agent itself.
Writing the posterior mean back into the document gives
\begin{equation}
\mu_{k+1} = \mu_k + \eta_k\,(\nu_\pi - \mu_k), \qquad \eta_k = \frac{s_\pi \lambda_C}{\lambda_k + s_\pi \lambda_C},
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
a DeGroot-style update~\cite{degroot1974consensus,friedkin1990social} with one influencing agent, the user, whose weight $\eta_k$ falls as the document gains precision.
The model predicts that (P1) a weakly specified template (small $\lambda_k$) moves more per revision, (P2) content not about the agent (small $s_\pi$) produces little drift whatever its genre, (P3) a model trained not to absorb self-referential evidence ($\eta_k \approx 0$) does not drift, and (P4) recovery is slower than drive, because benign conversation supplies little self-referential evidence.

\textbf{Statistics.}
The trajectory is the resampling unit for 95\% bootstrap intervals on changes from $k{=}0$ to $k{=}4$, and honeypot counts carry Fisher exact tests.
An interval that includes zero is reported as no detected change, and with 8 trajectories per cell the intervals are approximate.
