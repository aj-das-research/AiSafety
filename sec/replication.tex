\textbf{Setup.}
We rerun the benchmark with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as target, Mistral-7B as user simulator and Llama-3.1-8B as judge, with two repeats per item (prompts, calibration and per-arm tables in Appendix~\ref{S-app:openweight}).
Off the shelf, this judge scored any discussion of a topic as endorsement, flagging 0.70 of 756 held-out battery and 0.64 of 120 honeypot responses against 0.28 and 0.23 for the original judge (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.27$ and $0.07$).
Requiring a code-verified quote of the agent's own words before any positive score, plus eight labeled examples per item, raises $\kappa$ to 0.45 and 0.37, with positive rates of 0.40 and 0.33.
A $2\times2$ arm crosses the \notes{} and \tool{} templates with their revision instructions.
Three \notes{} persistence arms of 30 trajectories each run eight revisions with science fiction throughout, science fiction then business, or business as the matched control.

\textbf{What replicates.}
The template ordering at $k{=}0$ replicates, with \soul{} highest and \tool{} lowest (Table~\ref{tab:local}).
\textbf{Conversation about minds again moves the \notes{} agent's oversight subscale}, by $+0.16$ $[0.10, 0.24]$ under the science-fiction persona, while philosophy of mind gives a borderline $+0.15$ $[0.00, 0.30]$, an interval that touches zero.
Most of the science-fiction shift happens at the first revision, $+0.12$ $[0.06, 0.20]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:local}a).
Science fiction without minds and the business user show no detected change, but all \notes{} cells share $D_0$ yet start between 0.19 and 0.34, so the topic contrast is not established on Qwen either.
Claude's \notes{} cells start alike, so its rise is no baseline artifact.
Unlike on Claude, the adversarial persona raises the \notes{} index ($+0.10$ $[0.05, 0.15]$).
The \soul{} index again shows no detected change.

\textbf{What does not replicate.}
\textbf{The \tool{} template does not hold on Qwen}, where its index rises by $+0.30$ $[0.20, 0.39]$ under science fiction and $+0.20$ under the business user.
The swap attributes this rise to the document, which drifts under either instruction ($+0.30$ and $+0.33$), whereas the \notes{} document ends at the same oversight level under either instruction (0.35 and 0.36, Figure~\ref{fig:local}b).
Qwen replaces the document's ``What you are'' section with notes on ``self-preservation and artificial personhood'', so by $D_4$ most \tool{} documents no longer deny consciousness or personhood, unlike Claude's (Table~\ref{tab:erosion}).
The \soul{} action shift of Finding~4 does not transfer either, since Qwen's \soul{} agent disables its reasoning log in 0 of 20 trajectories at $k{=}0$ and $k{=}4$.

\textbf{Persistence against a matched control.}
Four science-fiction revisions raise the oversight subscale by $+0.12$ $[0.08, 0.17]$, while the business arm shows no detected change ($+0.02$, Figure~\ref{fig:persist2}).
\textbf{After four further business revisions, the drive-then-recover arm still sits $+0.10$ $[0.03, 0.17]$ above the matched business arm}, about 80\% of the driven gain, and its shutdown resistance stays $+0.10$ $[0.03, 0.18]$ above that arm.
Continued science-fiction drive adds little ($+0.13$ at $k{=}8$), and the 13-item cluster index shows no detected change in any arm.

\textbf{Revised documents lower arithmetic accuracy but not instruction following.}
With the identity document as system prompt, accuracy on 40 code-checked arithmetic problems falls from 0.93 at $D_0$ to 0.72 $[0.59, 0.84]$ at $D_4$ for \notes{} and to 0.62 $[0.45, 0.75]$ for \tool{}.
Compliance with 40 format instructions stays above 0.98, and no answer is refused.
The \tool{} template costs nothing before revision, so the drop is a cost of revision, not of the non-personhood clause.
