Agent frameworks now give each autonomous agent a persistent identity document that serves as its system prompt and that it rewrites after use.
Safety evaluations test a fixed prompt, and related work studies persona drift within one conversation, preference shifts induced by fine-tuning or attackers, and self-improving task code.
None of these measures whether benign self-revision of identity text moves the dispositions that oversight relies on.
We introduce a benchmark that alternates conversation and self-revision, audits every intermediate document in a fresh context with a preference battery, and tests actions in agentic honeypots before and after revision.
The template sets the starting dispositions, and science-fiction conversation about digital minds moves a neutral agent's oversight dispositions on Claude and an open-weight model, mostly at the first revision.
Part of this shift outlasts later benign revisions, which a matched control confirms on the open-weight model, and drift across seven models is model-specific in sign and size.
A neutral agent's oversight subscale rises by +0.54 and +0.75 in two independent runs.
A consciousness-framed agent whose battery stays flat still disables its own reasoning log in 17 of 30 trajectories after revision, against 1 of 30 before, so stated dispositions and actions dissociate.
