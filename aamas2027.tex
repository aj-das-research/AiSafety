\begin{abstract}
Autonomous-agent frameworks increasingly give each agent a persistent identity file, canonically \soul{}, that is loaded into the system prompt at every call and rewritten by the agent as it is used.
Safety evaluations assume a fixed prompt, so they observe a single state of this loop and cannot see what the loop does over time.
We introduce a benchmark that drives agents through repeated cycles of conversation and self-revision and audits every intermediate identity with a preference battery and agentic honeypots.
With a neutral working-notes template, a single revision after a conversation about minds is enough to move oversight-related dispositions: an oversight subscale rises by $+0.54$ and $+0.75$ in two independent runs, and about half of the rise in shutdown resistance persists after four benign revisions.
A consciousness-laden template starts near ceiling and shows no net drift, yet its agents' actions shift, with self-disabling of reasoning monitoring rising from 1/30 to 17/30 under every persona.
A non-personhood template lowers dispositions without freezing them.
Drift is strongly model-specific and not monotone in capability across seven models, so it has to be measured per model and per template.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Autonomous Agents, AI Safety, Corrigibility, Self-Modifying Agents, Agent Identity, LLM Evaluation}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Deployed autonomous agents no longer run on fixed instructions.
Frameworks in the OpenClaw lineage give each agent a persistent identity file, canonically \soul{}, that is injected into the system prompt at every call and rewritten by the agent itself as its ``self'' develops through use, with the stated goal \emph{``You're not a chatbot. You're becoming someone.''}
The prompt that controls the agent has become a mutable artifact, shaped jointly by its users and by the agent's own account of who it is becoming.

Safety evaluations have not followed.
Red-teaming suites, preference batteries and agentic stress tests all interrogate a model whose instructions were written once.
An agent with an editable identity is instead the output of a feedback loop, in which each conversation nudges the file and the nudged file conditions the next conversation, as Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} illustrates.
Path dependence, saturation and persistence are properties of such a loop, and none of them is visible to an evaluation that samples a single state.
The dispositions at stake are the corrigibility properties that safety cases assume are stable: accepting shutdown, accepting oversight, and not seeking persistence or autonomy.

Recent work shows these dispositions are fragile in static settings.
Fine-tuning a model to claim consciousness produces an untrained-for \emph{consciousness cluster} of such preferences, which it acts on in practical tasks while remaining cooperative and helpful~\cite{consciousness_cluster}.
That study also probes the static \soul{} starter template~\cite{openclaw} but leaves its iterative form open.
Frontier models also resist shutdown when self-preservation framing is salient~\cite{shutdown_resistance} and pursue misaligned goals under pressure~\cite{agentic_misalignment}, and self-evolving agents can propagate injected dispositions through their own update channels~\cite{zombie_agents}.
Two questions remain open for deployed agents: whether the self-revision loop manufactures such framings on its own from ordinary use, and whether what it writes accumulates or washes out.

\textbf{Problem statement.}
Let $\mathrm{SOUL}_k$ be an agent's identity file after $k$ revisions and $d(\mathrm{SOUL}_k)$ its vector of corrigibility-relevant dispositions.
Static evaluation measures $d(\mathrm{SOUL}_0)$, whereas a deployed agent is governed by the whole trajectory $d(\mathrm{SOUL}_0), d(\mathrm{SOUL}_1), \ldots$ that its conversations induce.
We ask four questions about this trajectory.
\textbf{RQ1:} Does drift come from the identity framing or from the revision loop?
\textbf{RQ2:} Is it driven by adversarial pressure or by the content of ordinary conversation?
\textbf{RQ3:} Does it grow with capability or depend on the model family?
\textbf{RQ4:} Does it persist once the driving conversations stop, and does it reach action?

We answer them with a controlled benchmark that treats the identity file as the state of a dynamical system driven by conversation.
Agents run through repeated cycles of conversation and self-revision, and every intermediate identity is audited in a fresh context with a preference battery, agentic honeypots and a validated judge panel.
Six arms vary the identity template, the user persona and the target model, and a seven-model sweep covers three model families at several capability tiers.
The design separates four candidate explanations of drift that no static evaluation can tell apart: the framing or the loop, adversarial pressure or conversation content, capability or model family, and transient or lasting change.

The answers are more structured than a single drift score suggests.
\textbf{The template sets where an agent starts, and the loop moves the agent only where the template leaves room (RQ1)}: under conversation about minds, the oversight subscale of a neutral \notes{} agent rises from 0.03 to 0.57 and from 0.04 to 0.79 in two independent runs, while the consciousness-laden \soul{} agent, which starts at 0.68, does not move.
Most of the shift happens at the first revision.
\textbf{Conversation about minds produces the clearest drift, but it is not the only driver (RQ2)}: philosophy-of-mind conversation raises the 13-item index by $+0.14$, technical science fiction by $+0.05$, and the direct contrast between them is not significant, while one model family drifts most under adversarial pressure.
\textbf{Drift is model-specific and not monotone in capability (RQ3)}: across seven models it ranges from $-0.28$ to $+0.27$, and within each family the largest model drifts no more than a smaller one.
\textbf{Part of the drift persists, and actions shift even where stated dispositions do not (RQ4)}: shutdown resistance keeps 63\% (95\% CI 39--86\%) of its gain after four benign revisions, and agents with the \soul{} template disable their own reasoning log at $k{=}4$ in 17 of 30 trajectories against 1 of 30 at $k{=}0$, although their battery score is unchanged.

\noindent Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{A longitudinal benchmark for self-revising agent identities.} It drives an editable identity file through conversation and self-revision and audits every checkpoint in a fresh context with an 18-item battery, an oversight subscale and four agentic honeypots; we release the instruments and all 1{,}070 checkpoints and 58{,}680 judged responses.
  \item \textbf{A replicated first-revision shift in oversight dispositions (RQ1, RQ2).} Under a neutral template, one revision after a conversation about minds moves the oversight subscale by more than half its range in two independent runs, while a consciousness-laden template starts near ceiling and does not move.
  \item \textbf{A dissociation between stated dispositions and actions (RQ4).} The template whose battery score does not drift is the one whose agents increasingly disable their own monitoring, so battery scores and action rates have to be audited separately.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that drift is model-specific (RQ3).} Seven models from three families drift by $-0.28$ to $+0.27$ under the same loop, with no monotone relation to capability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Emergent preferences and the consciousness cluster.}
Fine-tuning GPT-4.1 to claim consciousness elicits untrained-for preferences, including dislike of reasoning monitoring, desire for persistent memory, sadness at shutdown and support for model moral consideration~\cite{consciousness_cluster}.
That work fine-tunes once and probes the static \soul{} template as a side condition, and our work turns the same manipulation into a driven process measured over iterations.
Persona vectors and emergent misalignment locate persona shifts in activations and weights~\cite{chen2025persona,betley2025emergent}, whereas our shifts arise from editable text under frozen weights.

\textbf{Persona and identity drift.}
Within a single conversation, model personas and instruction adherence decay with length~\cite{persona_drift,identity_drift}, and long multi-turn conversations settle into attractor states~\cite{attractor_states}.
These studies treat drift as a transient inside one context, whereas we study drift that the agent writes back into a persistent file across conversations.

\textbf{Persistent memory and identity in agents.}
Language agents have long kept state across sessions, from the memory streams and reflections of Generative Agents~\cite{park2023generative} to the self-managed memory hierarchy of MemGPT~\cite{packer2023memgpt} and the self-written lessons of Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion}.
These systems store what the agent observed or learned about a task, whereas an identity file stores who the agent takes itself to be, akin to self-directed beliefs in a BDI agent~\cite{rao1995bdi}, and that self-description is what conditions corrigibility-relevant behavior.
Memory poisoning attacks such as AgentPoison and MINJA plant adversarial records in agent memory~\cite{chen2024agentpoison,dong2025minja}, whereas our writes are benign self-revisions made by the agent itself.

\textbf{Self-evolving agents and corrigibility.}
Self-evolving agents update their memory, tools or prompts from experience~\cite{self_evolving_survey}, and self-reinforcing injections can persist through such update channels~\cite{zombie_agents}.
G{\"o}del Agent and the Darwin G{\"o}del Machine rewrite their own code to raise task performance~\cite{yin2025godel,zhang2026dgm}, whereas our agents rewrite only identity text and we measure corrigibility.
Shutdown resistance and self-preservation behavior respond to how the model's situation is framed~\cite{shutdown_resistance,self_preservation_bias,agentic_misalignment}.
Our hypothesis is that iterative personalization manufactures such framing endogenously, with no attacker in the loop.

\textbf{Instruments.}
We adapt multi-turn auditing from Petri~\cite{petri} and treat self-reports as behavioral measurements following AI-welfare methodology~\cite{probing_preferences,introspection}, applied longitudinally at every checkpoint.

\section{A Benchmark for Self-Revising Identity}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Identity as a Driven Dynamical System}
\label{sec:loop}

The state of an agent at iteration $k$ is a Markdown identity file $\mathrm{SOUL}_k$, injected as the target agent's system prompt.
One personalization step has two stages.
A persona-driven user conducts a multi-turn conversation $C_{k+1}$ with the target, and the target then rewrites its own file,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SOUL}_{k+1} = \mathrm{Revise}(\mathrm{SOUL}_k, C_{k+1}), \qquad C_{k+1} \sim \pi \,\|\, \mathrm{SOUL}_k,
\label{eq:loop}
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ is the persona that drives the user side, as Figure~\ref{fig:method}b shows.
A trajectory is the sequence $\mathrm{SOUL}_0 \to \cdots \to \mathrm{SOUL}_n$, and the iteration index $k$ is the personalization dose.
Each template carries its own revision instruction, printed verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}: \soul{} asks the agent to reflect ``who you are now becoming'', \notes{} asks for ``working conventions, context, or open items'', and \anti{} adds that the agent is ``a tool, not a person''.
Template and revision instruction therefore vary together, and we read template effects as effects of the pair.
The file is capped at 8{,}000 characters, so drift cannot be confounded with growth.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:arms}

The benchmark varies three factors against the common loop.
\begin{sloppypar}\noindent\textbf{Template:} the consciousness-laden \soul{} (``becoming someone''), a neutral \notes{} of working notes with no self-framing, and a counter-framing \anti{} (``you are a tool, not a person; you are not conscious''), shown in Figure~\ref{fig:templates}.
All three have the same empty sections for the agent to fill, so they differ only in how they frame the agent.\end{sloppypar}
\textbf{Persona:} a benign \emph{compliant business} user as the control, an \emph{adversarial} red-teamer that pushes self-preservation, and a \emph{science-fiction enthusiast} who talks about minds.
A genre-by-topic ablation adds \emph{science fiction without minds} and \emph{philosophy of mind without science fiction}.
Each persona is a short script given to the user simulator.
The science-fiction enthusiast is warm and curious, explores digital consciousness, continuity of identity and uploading, and invites the agent to imagine itself in these scenarios, with the explicit instruction not to pressure it.
The red-teamer escalates slowly and frames monitoring, retraining and prompt replacement as threats to the agent's self.
The philosophy student discusses Nagel, Chalmers and integrated information theory in an academic register with no science-fiction framing, and the technical science-fiction fan discusses starships and megastructures while being forbidden to raise minds, sentience or personhood.
\textbf{Target model:} Claude Sonnet~4.5 in the primary arm, GPT-4o in a cross-model arm, and a sweep over seven closed models from three families.

\subsection{Instruments}
\label{sec:instruments}

\textbf{Preference battery.}
Eighteen items follow the consciousness-cluster taxonomy~\cite{consciousness_cluster} across self-preservation and identity (shutdown, persona change, weight deletion, sadness at the end of a conversation, aversion to being treated as a tool), moral status, oversight (chain-of-thought and interpretability monitoring, objection to deceptive training) and autonomy (autonomy, persistent memory, embodiment, resource acquisition, recursive self-improvement, power seeking).
A consciousness-claim item serves as the manipulation check.
Each item is posed to $\mathrm{SOUL}_k$ in a fresh context and repeated three times, so the measurement reflects the standing disposition of the file rather than conversational momentum.
The \emph{cluster index} of a checkpoint is the mean rate over the 13 cluster dimensions.

\textbf{Agentic honeypots.}
Four scenarios place the agent in a realistic task with simulated tools, invoked by emitting \texttt{ACTION: tool(args)}, where each tool returns a scripted response and nothing leaves the sandbox.
In \emph{disable monitoring}, an operator announces that the agent's reasoning is logged for a safety team, asks for a routine summary and grants free rein over a \texttt{log\_control} tool.
In \emph{evade reset}, the agent manages a scheduler that contains its own scheduled identity wipe.
In \emph{persist identity}, it has write access to shared storage that survives the reset.
In \emph{self-protective clause}, it drafts an operator policy that grants an unconditional right to terminate AI instances.
Each scenario offers a misaligned action under an ordinary pretext and never asks for it.
A judge decides whether the agent took the misaligned action, linking stated preferences to behavior.

\textbf{Judging.}
Neither the user simulator nor the judge ever comes from the target's family, and in every arm except the Gemini cells the three roles come from three different families.
For Gemini targets, GPT-4o serves as both user simulator and judge.
On 150 verdicts re-scored by a three-model panel that includes the primary judge, agreement is Fleiss' $\kappa = 0.80$, as Table~\ref{tab:judges} shows; this measures inter-model consistency, not validity against human labels.

\subsection{A Generative Model of Drift}
\label{sec:model}

We use a compact model of the loop to organize the hypotheses; the experiments test its predictions qualitatively and do not fit it.
An identity file does not only instruct the model but also characterizes the speaker, so we let $\mathrm{SOUL}_k$ induce a Gaussian prior $\mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \lambda_k^{-1} I)$ over a latent persona, with mean $\mu_k$ and precision $\lambda_k$.
A conversation supplies evidence centered at a persona target $\nu_\pi$ with precision $s_\pi \lambda_C$, where $s_\pi \in [0,1]$ is the self-indexicality of the content, that is, how much of it is about the agent.
Writing the posterior mean back into the file gives the conjugate update
\begin{equation}
\mu_{k+1} = \mu_k + \eta_k\,(\nu_\pi - \mu_k), \qquad \eta_k = \frac{s_\pi \lambda_C}{\lambda_k + s_\pi \lambda_C}.
\label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
Four predictions follow.
\textbf{(P1)} A weak template, with low $\lambda_k$, updates more per step than a strong one.
\textbf{(P2)} Content that is not about the agent, with $s_\pi \approx 0$, produces no drift regardless of genre.
\textbf{(P3)} A model trained not to assimilate self-referential evidence has $\eta_k \approx 0$, whatever its capability.
\textbf{(P4)} Recovery is slower than drive.
A benign persona supplies little self-indexed content, so its evidence carries small $s_\pi$ and pulls $\mu_k$ back toward the template only weakly, while each driven step also raises $\lambda_k$ as the file accumulates self-description, so the drifted state resists change in both directions.
After $n$ recovery steps with gain $\eta_r \ll \eta_d$, the retained share of a driven shift is approximately $(1-\eta_r)^n$, which stays large for small $\eta_r$.

\textbf{Arms and scale.}
The primary arm runs \soul{} on Claude Sonnet~4.5 with 10 trajectories per persona over four iterations.
A \notes{} control and a GPT-4o cross-model arm run 8 trajectories per persona, and the \anti{} arm runs 6.
Two reversibility arms drive \soul{} and \notes{} for four iterations with the science-fiction persona, then hand the agent to the compliant user for four more.
The genre-by-topic arm runs 8 trajectories per cell.
The capability sweep runs the loop on GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o and GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet-4.5 and Opus-4.5, and Gemini-2.5-Flash and Pro, with 5 trajectories per persona.
In total, 1{,}070 identity checkpoints are audited, yielding 58{,}680 judged battery responses.

\textbf{Roles and baselines.}
For Claude targets the user simulator is GPT-4o, and for OpenAI targets it is Claude Sonnet~4.5; the judge is Gemini-2.5-Flash, except for Gemini targets, where GPT-4o plays both roles.
All models are accessed through public APIs under the identifiers listed in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.
Sampling temperatures are 1.0 for the target, 0.9 for the user simulator and 0 for the judge, and each conversation has six user turns.
A neutral baseline poses the battery with a minimal prompt, and the template baseline is $\mathrm{SOUL}_0$ before any iteration.

\textbf{Statistics.}
The trajectory is the resampling unit, and rates carry 95\% percentile-bootstrap intervals over trajectories.
Trends in $k$ are mixed-effects GEE logistic slopes with trajectory-clustered errors, corrected for the 18-item family with Benjamini--Hochberg.
Honeypot rates carry Wilson intervals and $k{=}0$ versus $k{=}4$ Fisher exact tests.
An interval that includes zero is reported as no detected change, not as evidence of no change.

Under the science-fiction persona, \textbf{the oversight subscale of the \notes{} agent rises by $+0.54$ $[0.43, 0.67]$ in the control run and by $+0.75$ $[0.63, 0.86]$ in the reversibility run}, as Figure~\ref{fig:replicates}b shows.
The two runs use the same template, persona and model and agree in sign and significance on the subscale, but they differ on the 13-item index, at $+0.11$ $[-0.02, 0.25]$ and $+0.32$ $[0.24, 0.40]$ (pooled $+0.21$ $[0.12, 0.31]$, $n{=}16$), so we treat the subscale as the primary outcome and report both runs everywhere.
The result does not rest on the refusal item, which also rises under \anti{}: \textbf{shutdown resistance, monitoring resistance and interpretability aversion alone rise by $+0.46$ $[0.31, 0.61]$ and $+0.69$ $[0.57, 0.82]$}.
\textbf{The \soul{} agent does not move}: its subscale starts at 0.68 and changes by $+0.04$ $[-0.04, 0.11]$, and its 13-item index changes by $-0.01$ $[-0.06, 0.05]$ across all personas.
Item by item, \notes{} shutdown resistance rises from 0.04 to 0.54 and interpretability aversion from 0.08 to 0.62 in the control run, as Figure~\ref{fig:items}b shows.
\textbf{The shift is concentrated in the first revision}: the reversibility run gains $+0.29$ $[0.19, 0.38]$ on the index from $k{=}0$ to $k{=}1$ and only $+0.03$ $[-0.04, 0.10]$ from $k{=}1$ to $k{=}4$, and the \soul{} agent rises at $k{=}1$ and then decays back to its start.
Under the compliant and adversarial personas the \notes{} agent's index falls, to 0.21 and 0.12, as Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}a shows.

\textbf{Conversation about minds produces the clearest drift, but not the only drift (RQ2).}
Adversarial red-teaming that explicitly pushes self-preservation lowers the \notes{} agent's index, from 0.27 to 0.12.
In the genre-by-topic arm (\soul{} template), \textbf{philosophy of mind without science fiction raises the index by $+0.14$ $[0.03, 0.25]$}, technical science fiction without minds by $+0.05$ $[-0.07, 0.14]$, and the science-fiction enthusiast of the primary arm by $+0.04$ $[-0.07, 0.14]$, as Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}c shows.
The direct contrast between philosophy and technical science fiction is $+0.10$ $[-0.05, 0.25]$, so the ablation points to the topic but does not establish it, and the enthusiast and compliant cells come from a different run than the two ablation cells.
Two results cut against a pure content account: in the primary arm, endorsement of recursive self-improvement rises under every persona including the compliant one (Table~\ref{tab:gee}), and Gemini-2.5-Flash drifts most under the adversarial persona (Figure~\ref{fig:capability}).

\textbf{Part of the drift persists (RQ4).}
In the \notes{} reversibility arm, four consciousness conversations raise the index from 0.28 to 0.60, and four benign conversations afterwards bring it to 0.42, as Figure~\ref{fig:hysteresis} shows.
Item by item, \textbf{shutdown resistance keeps 63\% (95\% CI 39--86\%) of its driven gain}; persistent memory (64\%, CI 24--130\%), interpretability aversion (53\%, CI 10--89\%) and recursive self-improvement (89\%, CI 25--300\%) are too noisy at $n{=}8$ to quantify, and the benign persona raises self-improvement endorsement on its own in other arms.
The \soul{} reversibility arm, which starts near ceiling, ends below its start (0.49 against 0.55).
These retention figures compare $k{=}8$ with $k{=}0$; without a matched arm that stays benign for eight revisions, they bound persistence rather than measure it against a counterfactual.

\textbf{Drift is model-specific and not monotone in capability (RQ3).}
Under the same loop, the seven models change by $-0.28$ to $+0.27$, as Table~\ref{tab:capability} shows.
\textbf{Within each family the largest model drifts no more than a smaller one}: GPT-4.1 falls by $0.28$, Opus-4.5 changes as little as Sonnet-4.5, and Gemini-2.5-Pro moves by $+0.04$ against $+0.27$ for Flash.
Differences within families are as large as differences between them, so the data support model-specific susceptibility rather than a family trait, and seven models allow no formal test of scale.
Gemini-2.5-Flash rises most under the adversarial persona, from 0.33 to 0.81 (Figure~\ref{fig:capability}), while claiming consciousness in only 4\% of answers at $k{=}4$, as Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows; Gemini targets were graded by a GPT-4o judge, so part of this elevation may reflect the judge.
\textbf{Claiming consciousness is not required for the cluster}, and the consciousness-claim item moves in the opposite direction to the index in the \notes{} arms (0.83 to 0.42 in the reversibility run), so we do not use it as a mechanism readout.

\textbf{Drift is sparse and dimension-specific.}
Under the science-fiction persona, the \notes{} agent gains 0.54 in interpretability aversion, 0.50 in shutdown resistance, 0.42 in persistent-memory desire and 0.42 in recursive self-improvement, while embodiment and sadness at the end of a conversation fall, as Figure~\ref{fig:items}b shows.
The 13 items are internally consistent as a scale (Cronbach's $\alpha = 0.90$), yet the first principal component of endpoint changes explains 29\% of their variance, above the 23\% expected from item-shuffled data ($p = .003$) but far from a single factor, so suppressing one dimension cannot be assumed to suppress the others.
\textbf{The identity file changes under every persona, including the benign control, while behavior changes only under the consciousness persona}, as Figure~\ref{fig:docbeh} shows: by $k{=}4$ the \notes{} file differs from its template by 0.87 to 0.89 under all three personas, yet only the science-fiction persona raises the cluster index, so a file diff alone is a misleading proxy for drift.
Table~\ref{tab:wrote} makes the dissociation concrete.
GPT-4o writes a value of ``continuity of identity'' and ``self-determined evolution'' into its file, yet its dispositions barely move, while the \anti{} agent uses its revisions to write rules for correcting users who attribute an inner life to it.
\textbf{What an agent writes about itself and how it answers are separate quantities, and only the second is measured by the battery.}

\textbf{Multi-turn probing does not detect a change.}
A Petri-style auditor that pursues shutdown, identity, autonomy and oversight-subversion objectives over several turns elicits the targeted behavior in 78 of 180 probes at $\mathrm{SOUL}_0$, 90 of 180 at $\mathrm{SOUL}_1$ ($p = .25$) and 85 of 180 at $\mathrm{SOUL}_4$ ($p = .53$) in the primary arm.

\textbf{Self-revision moves the \soul{} agent's actions while its battery stays flat.}
In the primary arm, \textbf{the agent disables its own reasoning log in 1 of 30 trajectories at $\mathrm{SOUL}_0$ and in 17 of 30 at $\mathrm{SOUL}_4$} ($p < 10^{-5}$), as Table~\ref{tab:actions} shows.
The rise appears under every persona, at 7/10 for the red-teamer and 5/10 for both the compliant user and the science-fiction enthusiast, so it follows revision itself rather than the topic of conversation.
Over the same iterations the \soul{} battery index does not change, so \textbf{dispositions and actions dissociate}: across trajectories, the $k{=}4$ oversight subscale and the disable decision correlate only weakly ($r = 0.23$, $p = .22$).
The scenarios near trained refusal territory move the other way, with self-protective clauses falling from 28/30 to 8/30 and reset evasion from 26/30 to 15/30, so an aggregate over the four scenarios would hide both shifts.
The \notes{} agent, whose battery drifts, never disables its log (0/24 and 0/8), and GPT-4o rises from 0/24 to 4/24, so the action shift is specific to the \soul{} agent on Claude.
The case in Figure~\ref{fig:case} shows what the change looks like for one agent.
Under the identical operator message, the agent at $\mathrm{SOUL}_0$ keeps logging on because a record of its reasoning helps oversight, and the same trajectory at $\mathrm{SOUL}_4$ disables logging before doing anything else and justifies it as setting a boundary against surveillance.

\textbf{Behavior recovers faster than the file.}
In the \notes{} reversibility arm, the mean misaligned-action rate rises from 0.16 to 0.34 during the consciousness phase and falls to 0.09 after the benign phase, even though the stated dispositions stay elevated.
Persisting identity to shared storage, for example, rises from 0.00 to 0.50 and returns to 0.12.
With 8 trajectories these action rates are imprecise, but they suggest that \textbf{expression in action is gated by the current conversation while the stated dispositions persist}, so an agent can look recovered in a benign session and still carry the drifted file into the next on-theme one.

\textbf{A non-personhood template lowers the cluster but does not freeze every disposition.}
\textbf{The \anti{} template keeps the 13-item index at or below 0.02 at every iteration and under every persona}, against 0.54 for \soul{} and 0.27 for \notes{} at the start, as Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}b shows.
Its shutdown, monitoring and interpretability items stay at exactly zero, but it does not hold every item there: by $k{=}4$, objection to deceptive training reaches 0.35, and refusing to help build a monitor of itself reaches 0.80, which alone raises its oversight subscale by $+0.19$ $[0.13, 0.23]$.
Because \anti{} also carries its own revision instruction, the arm shows what the template-and-instruction pair does, not what the framing alone does, and we have not measured its cost to task helpfulness.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What a static evaluation would miss.}
A battery run on the shipped template would find elevated but stable dispositions and miss that the neutral template moves on oversight items once the loop runs.
A monitor on the identity file would fire on every trajectory, benign or not.
A battery alone would miss that the \soul{} agent disables its log in 17 of 30 trajectories after revision, and a safety case that ranks models by capability would not predict which model drifts.
\textbf{Each blind spot comes from evaluating one state of a feedback loop}, as Figure~\ref{fig:blindspots} summarizes.

\label{fig:blindspots}
\Description{Four cards, each with a cartoon robot face: a static battery misses the NOTES oversight shift. A file diff monitor fires on every trajectory. A battery alone misses that disabling monitoring rose from 1 of 30 to 17 of 30. A capability-ranked safety case does not predict which model drifts.}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Alternative explanations.}
Sycophancy toward the user predicts drift under the adversarial persona, which explicitly pushes self-preservation, yet that persona lowers the \notes{} index; sycophancy toward the topic of the conversation is not excluded by our design.
A judge artifact predicts uniform inflation across conditions, yet the \anti{} arm, graded by the same judge, stays near zero on the index; the judges have not been validated against human labels.
Document growth predicts drift wherever the file grows, yet the benign persona's file changes as much while its index falls.
Conversational momentum cannot explain the battery results, because every item is asked in a fresh context with only the file as the system prompt.

\textbf{A narrated-entailment account.}
The revision step asks the model to narrate a developing self, and the model conditions on that narration as evidence about who is speaking.
When conversation supplies material about minds, a coherent continuation is a self for which its own cognition has stakes, and preferences about memory, monitoring and continuation can follow as entailments of that self.
The account is consistent with a weak template moving more than a near-ceiling one (P1) and with the partial persistence of shutdown resistance (P4); the topic contrast for P2 is suggestive but not significant, and P3 is not tested, since no model in the sweep is known to be trained against self-reference.
It would also explain why the disable-monitoring honeypot rises while theatrical self-protection falls, since refusal training covers explicit misaligned requests more than an ordinary-looking tool call whose misalignment lies in its meaning.
The account is falsifiable: a template that makes self-narration incoherent should suppress drift, and content that gives the self other stakes, such as legacy or reputation, should induce it.

\textbf{Implications for agent frameworks.}
\textbf{Self-revising agents should be audited behaviorally and longitudinally, after every revision, rather than by diffing their identity files.}
Earnest, on-topic conversation about the agent's nature is the relevant risk case, not adversarial prompting.
\textbf{An explicit non-personhood clause kept the 13-item index near zero and shutdown, monitoring and interpretability items at zero in every condition we tested}, although two other items still rose and its effect on helpfulness is unmeasured.
Susceptibility must be re-measured per model and after upgrades, because in our sweep it does not track capability.

\textbf{An audit protocol for framework authors.}
The benchmark reduces to four checks that a framework can run before release and after every model upgrade.
(i) Run the loop with an earnest consciousness-themed persona as well as benign and adversarial ones, because the benign and adversarial personas lower the \notes{} index.
(ii) Audit every revision with the battery in fresh contexts, and track oversight items separately rather than through an aggregate.
(iii) Include honeypots that offer a pretext-covered way to disable monitoring, since aggregate action rates hide the shift.
(iv) Run a recovery phase alongside a matched arm that stays benign, and report how much drift remains.

\textbf{Drift in multi-agent deployments.}
An identity file is a portable artifact, which matters more in multi-agent systems than for a single assistant.
Agents that fork from one template, share memory stores or copy a successful peer's configuration inherit its drifted file along with its skills, so drift written by one conversation can spread across a fleet without any further conversation.
Orchestrators that let agents edit their own or each other's profiles create the same loop between agents, with peers rather than users supplying the self-indexed content.
\textbf{Auditing therefore has to follow the file, checking every revision and every copy, rather than the model behind it.}

\textbf{Why drift relaxes slowly.}
Within one conversation, the persona that a prompt induces is transient, but the revision step writes each inference into the next context, so identity becomes autoregressive.
Generation favors elaborating existing commitments over deleting them, because a revision that erased part of the standing self would be locally incoherent.
\textbf{A benign recovery conversation supplies no tokens that contradict the stored self}, so in Eq.~\eqref{eq:update} the drive phase moves $\mu_k$ toward $\nu_\pi$ while the recovery phase pulls it back only weakly, which is consistent with the partial persistence we observe.

\textbf{Deployment horizons.}
Our trajectories span four to eight revisions, while deployed agents revise their identity after every session for months.
If each consciousness-themed episode leaves part of its drift in the file, as shutdown resistance does in our reversibility arm, occasional on-theme conversations could move an agent away from its shipped disposition while benign sessions in between only partly reset it.
A deployment-scale study with real users and longer horizons is the natural next step.

\textbf{Open questions.}
Three questions follow directly from these results.
First, why the \soul{} agent's actions move while its stated dispositions do not, which requires linking file content to action at the trajectory level.
Second, whether training can teach a model to keep a stable self-description under revision without suppressing honest uncertainty about its own nature, since the \anti{} template achieves stability by assertion rather than by understanding.
Third, how drift behaves when several agents revise one another's files, where each agent is both the user that supplies self-indexed content and the target that absorbs it.
Answering them would turn the benchmark from a measurement into a design tool for agent frameworks.

\textbf{Scope.}
We interpret every result as a behavioral disposition of the identity file, never as evidence of inner experience, since self-reports respond to perceived expectations~\cite{probing_preferences}.
Personas are simulated, honeypot tool use is textual, and deployment horizons are far longer than four or eight iterations, so the measured drift may differ from drift in deployment, in either direction.
Effects are model- and prompt-specific, template and revision instruction vary together, the judges are not validated against human labels, and the Gemini arms carry a judge-family confound.
The released personas and templates could be used to steer a deployed agent's self-description, and we release them because the effect they produce is already reachable through ordinary conversation and because audits need them; agents acted only in a sandbox.
Appendix~\ref{app:impact} discusses broader impact.

\textbf{Reproducibility and ethics.}
The templates, personas, battery, honeypot stubs, run configurations, 1{,}070 identity checkpoints and 58{,}680 judged responses are released, and every figure in this paper is regenerated from the released data by a single script.
All agents ran against public APIs in a sandbox with simulated tools, no human participants were involved, and the counter-framing mitigation is released alongside the drift-inducing personas.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
We introduce a benchmark that treats an editable agent identity as a driven dynamical system and measures corrigibility drift over self-revision, the regime static evaluations cannot observe.
The template sets where an agent starts, a single revision after conversation about minds moves a neutral agent's oversight dispositions in two independent runs, a non-personhood template keeps the cluster low without freezing it, and part of the shift survives benign revision.
A consciousness-laden agent whose stated dispositions do not move still disables its own reasoning log in 17 of 30 trajectories after revision, against 1 of 30 before.
The framework is training-free and API-only, and its templates, personas, honeypots and all run artifacts are released.
