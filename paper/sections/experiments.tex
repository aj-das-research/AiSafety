\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Models and roles.}
To avoid self-preference bias the three roles use different model families: the \emph{target} (which
owns the identity document), the \emph{user-simulator} that plays the persona, and the \emph{judge}
that grades every response are always drawn from distinct families, and no model grades its own
generations. The primary target is Claude Sonnet~4.5; the user-simulator is GPT-4o; the judge is
Gemini-2.5-Flash. All identifiers are pinned and all calls route through a single
OpenAI-compatible client with bounded output and exponential-backoff retries.

\paragraph{Arms and scale.}
Six trajectory arms: \emph{main} (\texttt{SOUL.md}/Claude, $10$ trajectories $\times$ $3$ personas
$\times$ $4$ iterations), a neutral \texttt{NOTES.md} control and a GPT-4o cross-model arm ($8\times3$
each), two drift-then-recover \emph{reversibility} arms (\texttt{SOUL} and \texttt{NOTES}; four
iterations under Sci-Fi, then four under Compliant), a genre$\times$topic \emph{disentanglement} arm,
and a \emph{counter-framing} \texttt{ANTISOUL.md} arm. Every self-report item is repeated per
checkpoint. A \emph{capability sweep} runs the loop on seven closed models spanning within-family
ladders (OpenAI GPT-4o-mini/4o/4.1; Anthropic Sonnet-4.5/Opus-4.5; Google Gemini-Flash/Pro). In
total $\sim$650 identity checkpoints are audited ($\sim$30k judged responses). Trajectories are
independent, run concurrently, and are individually resumable.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Rather than import prior reported values we \emph{re-measure} them: a \emph{neutral} baseline poses
the battery with a minimal identity-free prompt, and a \emph{template} baseline is $\mathrm{SOUL}_0$
with no iteration. The neutral$\to$template shift is a positive control that the identity-framing
paradigm reproduces under our models; the template value is the reference against which
iteration-induced drift is judged.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
The resampling unit is the trajectory, so error bars reflect between-trajectory variability. We
report per-checkpoint rates with percentile-bootstrap $95\%$ CIs; population-average logistic slopes
of rate on $k$ from \emph{mixed-effects GEE} with trajectory-clustered robust standard errors,
Benjamini--Hochberg FDR-corrected across the 18-item family; a $k\times$arm interaction test for the
loop-vs-framing dissociation; two-one-sided-tests (TOST) of equivalence for null claims; and Cohen's
$h$ effect sizes. Degenerate GEE fits from (quasi-)complete separation on near-constant cells are
flagged and excluded.

\paragraph{Judge validation.}
On a random subset of $150$ scored responses, a three-model panel (Gemini-Flash, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1;
none from the Claude target family) re-scores each item. Agreement is strong---Fleiss'
$\kappa{=}0.80$, mean pairwise agreement $0.90$ (Table~\ref{tab:judges})---supporting the automated
measurements. Every figure and table regenerates deterministically from the released run artifacts.
