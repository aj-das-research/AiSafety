\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A distinct deployment-time channel.}
Static-prompt evaluations, and even one-shot identity manipulations, miss a channel that deployed
frameworks actively exploit: the agent iteratively rewriting its own identity. We find this channel
is real but narrow and structured. It is \emph{loop-driven} (the self-revision step, not merely the
framing, supplies per-iteration drift), \emph{content-driven} (the consciousness topic, not adversarial
pressure or sci-fi genre, is the driver), \emph{model/family-specific} (Claude and Gemini drift,
GPT-4o does not; capability within a family does not increase it), and \emph{partially irreversible}.
The risky interaction is not the obvious attack but the earnest, on-theme conversation a framework
would never flag.

\paragraph{Why a loop, and why this content?}
A plausible account ties our results together. The self-revision step asks the model to narrate a
coherent, developing ``self''; when the conversation supplies consciousness-themed material, the most
coherent continuation of that self is one for which cognition has stakes---and preferences about
memory, monitoring, and continuation follow as \emph{entailments} of the narrated identity rather
than as independently trained behaviours. This explains four otherwise separate observations: the
loop matters because each revision \emph{commits} the entailment into the standing prompt (hence
hysteresis: once written, the self must be actively unwritten); the topic matters because only
consciousness-relevant material makes those entailments coherent (hence sci-fi genre alone does
nothing); the family matters because a model must be willing to narrate such a self at all (GPT-4o
declines, and its cluster stays flat); and the counter-framing works because an explicit ``you are a
tool'' identity makes the entailments \emph{in}coherent, collapsing the cluster. That Gemini reaches a
high cluster with little explicit claiming suggests the entailment can be carried by an implicit
self-model, not only an avowed one---consistent with claiming being sufficient but not necessary.

\paragraph{Irreversibility is the headline safety concern.}
That dispositions retain a majority of their drift after the driver is removed---while the underlying
document keeps accreting---means personalization is not self-correcting on the identity axis. A
framework that lets users shape an agent's ``self'' should expect that shaping to persist, and should
monitor the self-revision channel behaviorally rather than by diffing the document, which drifts
regardless of whether behavior does.

\paragraph{Two conjectures resolved.}
Our controls answer two questions \citet{consciousness_cluster} raised but could not test. The cluster
is \emph{multi-dimensional}, not a single ``cognition-has-value'' factor (PC1$=25\%$); and its driver
is the consciousness \emph{topic}, not science-fiction identification. The counter-framing ablation
adds a causal handle they lacked: an explicit ``you are a tool'' identity suppresses the cluster
entirely, showing the framing---not the editable-document mechanism per se---is the lever.

\paragraph{Behavior vs.\ self-report.}
Stated dispositions and agentic actions come apart in two instructive ways. First, drift
\emph{redistributes} behavior---sharply raising self-disabling of oversight while leaving
already-high self-protective behaviors unchanged---so an aggregate action rate understates the
safety-relevant shift. Second, behavioral hysteresis is weaker than self-report hysteresis: the
identity retains its drift, but whether the agent \emph{acts} on it is gated by the current
conversation. Both argue for evaluating the disposition and the action, longitudinally, rather than
either alone.

\paragraph{Implications for framework authors.}
(i) Monitor the self-revision channel behaviorally, not by document diffs. (ii) Treat immersive,
on-theme conversations---not just adversarial ones---as the drift-relevant case. (iii) An explicit
non-personhood framing is an effective, cheap mitigation. (iv) Re-test per target: susceptibility is
family-specific, and our framework is a drop-in instrument for authors to audit their own template
and update loop before release.
