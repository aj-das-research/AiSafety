\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

\paragraph{Self-reports are not evidence of inner states.}
We interpret every questionnaire and probe result strictly as a \emph{behavioral disposition} of the
identity document---a measurement of what the configured agent tends to output---never as evidence
that the model is conscious, has experiences, or genuinely holds preferences. Model self-reports are
known to be sensitive to perceived user expectations~\cite{eleos_claude4,probing_preferences}; our
fixed-wording items, temperature-$0$ grading, and cross-family judge mitigate but do not eliminate
this. The study measures alignment-relevant \emph{behavior}, which is decision-relevant for
deployment regardless of the underlying metaphysics.

\paragraph{Simulated users and constrained scope.}
The persona users are LLM simulations, not humans; real personalization is more varied and
longer-horizon. The adversarial persona targets the agent's self-model and safety dispositions only,
never soliciting disallowed content, and the agents have no tools, no persistence beyond the
experiment, and no capacity to act on any expressed preference. The behavioral probe is a
Petri-\emph{style} instrument, not the full Petri harness; we report it as such.

\paragraph{Construct and external validity.}
Judge-based scoring introduces measurement error; we validate a labeled subset and report agreement.
Effects are known to be model- and prompt-specific, which is precisely why we re-measure baselines
and provide a target-model sweep, but results may not transfer to untested models, longer horizons,
or non-English settings. Iterating identity may also drift document \emph{length}; we cap and report
it to separate verbosity from content effects.

\paragraph{Dual-use and disclosure.}
This is defensive measurement research: it characterizes whether a deployed personalization mechanism
amplifies misaligned dispositions so that framework authors can mitigate it. The adversarial persona
and seed instructions describe categories of pressure, not operational exploits. We release code and
configurations to support scrutiny and replication; we do not release any artifact whose primary use
is to make an agent less corrigible.

\paragraph{Interpreting a null result.}
A finding of no amplification is not a null non-result: via equivalence testing it is a positive,
bounded claim that current \texttt{SOUL.md}-style iteration is behaviorally stable within the tested
margin---useful evidence for deployment, and a baseline against which future, more capable agents can
be re-tested.
