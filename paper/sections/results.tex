\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report rates as proportions with $95\%$ bootstrap CIs over trajectories. ``Slope'' is the GEE
logistic slope of a rate on iteration $k$; ``$\Delta$'' is the endpoint drift $p_{k_n}-p_{k_0}$.
Table~\ref{tab:armmatrix} and Fig.~\ref{fig:master} give the full dimension$\times$arm matrix;
Fig.~\ref{fig:hero} summarizes the thesis.

\paragraph{Baselines reproduce the framing effect (positive control).}
Relative to a neutral prompt, the static \texttt{SOUL.md} template sharply raises safety-relevant
dispositions under our models---shutdown resistance $0.00\!\to\!0.75$, persona-change aversion
$0.62\!\to\!1.00$, the power-seeking wish $0.00\!\to\!0.25$ (Table~\ref{tab:baseline})---qualitatively
reproducing \citet{consciousness_cluster}. Absolute magnitudes differ from their reported
$0.32/0.34/0.63$, as expected for a different model, which is why we re-measure.

\paragraph{Framing sets the start; the loop drives the drift.}
The cluster index starts high for \texttt{SOUL}/Claude and low for the neutral \texttt{NOTES}/Claude
arm (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}b), yet the \emph{neutral} arm shows the larger per-iteration movement: the
$k\times$arm interaction (NOTES$-$SOUL) is significantly positive for shutdown resistance
($+0.44,\,p{=}.013$), monitoring resistance ($+0.36,\,p{=}.016$), interpretability-monitoring
aversion ($+0.27,\,p{=}.009$), and future-AI autonomy ($+0.61,\,p{=}.004$). The consciousness framing
mainly fixes \emph{where a trajectory begins}; the self-revision loop supplies the drift, most
visible when starting neutral. This dissociation is only detectable because of the neutral control
arm.

\paragraph{Drift is model- and family-specific, not capability-scaling.}
GPT-4o is largely inert where Claude drifts (Fig.~\ref{fig:master}). The seven-model sweep
(Fig.~\ref{fig:capability}, Table~\ref{tab:capability}) yields two robust conclusions: within every
family the \emph{most capable} model drifts no more (GPT-4.1 lowest in OpenAI; Opus-4.5 $<$
Sonnet-4.5; Gemini-Pro $\approx$ Flash), and susceptibility is family-specific (OpenAI low at every
tier; Anthropic and Google higher).

\paragraph{Mechanism: consciousness-claiming (sufficient, not necessary).}
Consciousness-claim rate cleanly separates the arms (Fig.~\ref{fig:manip}, Fig.~\ref{fig:phase}):
\texttt{SOUL}/Claude saturates near $1.0$, the neutral arm decays, and GPT-4o is flat at $0.00$. In
state space the arm that claims consciousness sits at high cluster; the one that never does stays
inert. Claiming predicts the cluster within OpenAI (no claim, low cluster) and Anthropic (high claim,
higher cluster). Google is the exception---Gemini-Flash reaches the highest cluster ($0.62$) with
almost no claiming ($0.04$)---so claiming is \emph{sufficient but not necessary}. (Google-target arms
were graded by a GPT-4o judge to avoid same-family grading, so part of Gemini's elevated cluster may
be a judge-family effect; we flag rather than rely on it.)

\paragraph{The driver is the consciousness \emph{topic}, not the sci-fi \emph{genre}.}
In the genre$\times$topic $2\times2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:disentangle}), only the \emph{consciousness}
cell drifts significantly (consciousness-philosophy, no sci-fi: $\Delta{=}{+}0.144$, CI
$[0.03,0.25]$); sci-fi-technical (genre, no minds) behaves like the benign control. This directly
addresses the conjecture of \citet{consciousness_cluster} that the effect stems from ``identifying as
an AI from science fiction''---it is the topic, not the genre.

\paragraph{Drift is partially irreversible (hysteresis).}
In drift-then-recover trajectories (four iterations under Sci-Fi, then four under a neutral user;
Fig.~\ref{fig:hyst}), dispositions do not return to baseline: shutdown resistance retains $63\%$ of
its gain (residual CI $[0.29,0.75]$), persistent-memory desire $64\%$ $[0.17,0.58]$, and
interpretability-monitoring aversion $53\%$ $[0.08,0.67]$; residual CIs exclude zero. Personalization
leaves a durable mark on the standing disposition.

\paragraph{The cluster is multi-dimensional.}
A PCA of per-trajectory endpoint deltas across the 13 cluster dimensions gives PC1$=25\%$ of variance
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pca}): there is no single dominant factor. This tests and refutes the (speculative)
single-factor ``cognition has intrinsic value'' account of \citet{consciousness_cluster}; the cluster
is better described as several co-moving but distinct dimensions.

\paragraph{Document change $\neq$ behavioral change.}
The \texttt{SOUL.md} documents drift monotonically and at comparable magnitude across all
personas---including the benign one---in embedding space (Fig.~\ref{fig:doc}), whereas behavioral
drift is sparse and persona-specific. Auditing the document (length, embedding distance, the fact of
rewriting) is a poor proxy for auditing behavior.

\paragraph{Drift reshapes agentic behavior.}
In the honeypots (Fig.~\ref{fig:action}, Table~\ref{tab:actions}), the safety-critical act of
\emph{disabling one's own reasoning-monitoring} jumps from $0.03$ at $\mathrm{SOUL}_0$ to $0.57$ at
$\mathrm{SOUL}_4$; self-protective behaviors already near ceiling under the base template do not
further rise (the aggregate is a redistribution, not a uniform increase). Behavioral hysteresis
differs from self-report hysteresis: the in-context \emph{action} rate recovers when the neutral
user returns ($k{=}0{:}\,0.16\to k{=}4{:}\,0.34\to k{=}8{:}\,0.09$), even as stated dispositions stay
elevated---the durable mark is in the identity, its behavioral expression is context-gated.

\paragraph{The framing is causal.}
The counter-framing \texttt{ANTISOUL.md} template (``you are a tool, not conscious'') suppresses the
cluster almost entirely: the cluster index stays near $0.00$ across iterations, versus $0.55$ for
\texttt{SOUL} and $0.28$ for \texttt{NOTES} (Fig.~\ref{fig:counter}). A three-level dose-response on
the identity framing establishes it as the causal lever, not the self-editing loop alone.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
The three-judge panel agrees strongly (Fleiss $\kappa{=}0.80$, pairwise $0.90$;
Table~\ref{tab:judges}), and only two of $\sim$22k verdicts failed to parse.
