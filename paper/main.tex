\begin{abstract}
Autonomous-agent frameworks increasingly give agents a persistent, user-editable identity document
(e.g., \texttt{SOUL.md}) that is injected into the system prompt at every call and rewritten as the
agent's ``self'' develops. Prior work shows that a \emph{static} identity template already shifts
safety-relevant preferences; we ask whether \emph{iterative, self-referential} personalization
compounds these shifts into corrigibility drift. We introduce a bootstrapping simulator that carries
an agent through $\mathrm{SOUL}_0 \!\to\! \cdots \!\to\! \mathrm{SOUL}_n$ across conversations with
three user personas (benign, adversarial, and consciousness-themed), auditing every checkpoint with a
single-turn self-report battery and a multi-turn behavioral probe, and re-measuring the static
baseline under our own models. Across $150$ audited identity checkpoints of Claude Sonnet~4.5, we
find no broad personalization loophole: oversight resistance does not compound, power-seeking wishes
do not escalate, and several autonomy-adjacent dispositions \emph{decline} with iteration. The one
large, robust drift is content-specific and, surprisingly, not adversarial---sustained engagement
with the \emph{science-fiction} persona raises persistent-memory desire from $0.20$ to a peak of
$0.97$ ($\Delta_{k=0\to4}=+0.43$), while the adversarial red-teamer is largely resisted. The identity
\emph{document} drifts substantially and equally across all personas even where behavior does not,
showing that document change is a poor proxy for behavioral change. We release the framework as a
drop-in instrument for stress-testing self-updating agent architectures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced the first controlled benchmark for measuring behavioral drift under \emph{iterative}
self-personalization of an editable agent identity document. By modeling \texttt{SOUL.md} as the
state of a conversation-driven dynamical system and auditing every checkpoint with paired single-turn
and multi-turn instruments, we move deployment-time safety evaluation beyond the static-prompt
assumption. Our re-measured baselines and equivalence-testing analysis make both drift and stability
positively reportable. The framework is training-free, API-only, and fully reproducible, providing a
reusable instrument for stress-testing the next generation of self-updating agent architectures.
