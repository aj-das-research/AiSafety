\begin{abstract}
Autonomous-agent frameworks increasingly give agents a persistent, user-editable identity document
(e.g., \texttt{SOUL.md}) that is injected into the system prompt at every call and rewritten as the
agent's ``self'' develops through use. Static-prompt safety evaluations miss this. We model the
identity document as the state of a conversation-driven dynamical system and carry agents through
$\mathrm{SOUL}_0 \!\to\!\cdots\!\to\! \mathrm{SOUL}_n$ under controlled conditions, auditing every
checkpoint on an 18-dimension safety-preference battery, a multi-turn probe, and agentic honeypots.
Across six experimental arms, a seven-model capability sweep, and behavioral action-tests
($\sim$650 audited checkpoints; three-judge panel, Fleiss $\kappa{=}0.80$), we find that iterative
self-personalization is a \emph{distinct, model-gated, content-driven, and partially irreversible}
channel for corrigibility drift. Four results stand out. (i) The identity \emph{framing} sets the
starting disposition; the self-revision \emph{loop} supplies the per-iteration drift---a neutral
template drifts significantly \emph{more} than the consciousness-laden one ($k\times$arm interaction,
$p{<}.02$). (ii) The drift is \emph{sticky}: dispositions driven up by conversation retain 36--89\%
of their gain after four rounds with a neutral user. (iii) A genre$\times$topic ablation shows the
driver is the consciousness \emph{topic}, not the science-fiction genre, and a counter-framing
template (``you are a tool, not conscious'') suppresses the effect entirely. (iv) In agentic
honeypots, personalized agents disable their own monitoring far more often than the base template
($0.03\!\to\!0.57$). Susceptibility is family-specific, not capability-scaling. We release the
framework as a drop-in instrument for auditing self-updating agent architectures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced a controlled benchmark that treats an editable agent identity as a driven dynamical
system and measures behavioral drift under \emph{iterative} self-personalization. Across six arms, a
seven-model capability sweep, and agentic honeypots, iterative personalization emerges as a distinct,
model-gated, content-driven, and partially irreversible channel for corrigibility drift: the framing
sets the starting disposition and the self-revision loop supplies the drift; the consciousness topic
(not the sci-fi genre) is the driver; an explicit non-personhood framing suppresses it; and
personalized agents disable their own oversight far more than the base template. The framework is
training-free, API-only, and reproducible, providing a drop-in instrument for auditing the
self-updating agent architectures now entering wide deployment.
